Privacy policy

Oct 5, 2026 · Orion Lab Studios

The short version

  • We keep no personal information about your child or you. The game never asks for a name, email, photo or voice. It asks for an age range once, and keeps the answer on the device.
  • There are no accounts, no chat and no purchases, no analytics of our own, and we do no tracking.
  • The Android app offers optional reward videos from Google AdMob. They are non-personalised, child-directed for players under 13, and parents can switch them off. When a video is requested, Google receives device and ad information, described under Reward videos. The iPhone and iPad app offers the same optional reward videos from Kidoz, an ad network made for children's apps that shows contextual ads only.
  • Progress and learning stats are saved only on your child's device. Nothing is sent to us.
  • You can erase everything the game has saved, at any time, from the For Parents page in the game.

This policy covers the Spell Shore game made by Orion Lab Studios, in every version we release (iPhone, iPad and Android), and this website's Spell Shore pages. "The parents' page" below means the For Parents page inside the game, behind the grown-up question.

Who we are

Spell Shore is made by Orion Lab Studios ("we", "us"). Contact us about privacy at orionlabstudios@gmail.com. We answer within 30 days.

Spell Shore has no accounts or logins, no cloud saves, no analytics or crash reporting of its own, no in-app purchases, no chat, no multiplayer, no leaderboards, no links to other websites or social media, and no notifications. It does not use the microphone, camera or location services. It never reads the device's advertising ID for any player, on Android or iPhone (the Android app does not have the permission), and it does not show Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt because it does not track anyone.

There are three exceptions to "nothing leaves the game": when a video is requested, the ad software exchanges data with its provider (Google on Android, Kidoz on iPhone and iPad); a tapped ad video can open the app store or the advertiser's page; and "Report an ad", behind the grown-up question, opens your email app. For adult players in the EU and UK, Google's consent message can also contain links to Google's and its partners' privacy pages.

Getting the game

If you download Spell Shore from Apple's App Store or Google Play, the store handles the download under its own privacy policy. We do not receive information about you from the store beyond anonymous totals the stores show every developer.

Everything the game needs, including its graphics and font, is built into the app. Apart from the Android reward videos and Google's consent message described below, the game does not contact any server.

Children's privacy

Spell Shore is made for children. We follow children's privacy laws, including the US Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA), the EU and UK GDPR, and the UK Age Appropriate Design Code.

  • We keep nothing about your child. Spell Shore stores everything on the device and sends nothing to us, and we never sell data.
  • The ad service does receive some data. On Android, the ad software inside the app sends Google the device information listed under Reward videos, such as the IP address and device identifiers. Laws like COPPA count that as personal information. For players under 13, every ad request is marked as child-directed, so Google uses this information only to show and count the ad, limit how often ads repeat, and prevent fraud, never for personalised ads or to build a profile. Requests for players under 18 are also marked as coming from someone under the age of consent.
  • The age answer only makes ads stricter. The game asks everyone the same neutral question. A younger answer switches on the child-directed and under-age settings; whatever the answer, ads stay non-personalised. The age itself is never sent to anyone.
  • The parents' page is behind a grown-up question so children don't wander into it. There are no analytics of our own and no social features.

Reward videos (Google AdMob on Android, Kidoz on iPhone and iPad)

The game offers optional reward videos: from Google AdMob on Android, and from Kidoz on iPhone and iPad. The first test versions may have none. The rules are the same on every device:

  • Only if the player chooses. Nothing pops up by itself and nothing interrupts play: a video plays only when the player taps a "watch a video" button, and the video that plays is an ad from Google or Kidoz, not part of the game. That button is offered for a bonus prize (at most 4 offers a day), or on the hint button when a player is stuck on a word and has used the free hints; after that video, the game shows the whole word for a few seconds (at most 10 a day). There are no banners and no pop-up ads.
  • Closing a video. Every video has a close button (X). Some videos show it only after a few seconds. Closing early simply means no bonus.
  • Non-personalised for everyone. Ads are never based on your child's interests or on tracking them across apps or websites. Players under 13 get child-directed, G-rated ads only.
  • Provided by Google AdMob (the Google Mobile Ads SDK), which is part of Google Play's Families Self-Certified Ads SDK program. The game starts it only when a video is requested, never while videos are switched off, and loads nothing in advance.
  • What Google receives. Each time a video is requested (even if no ad is shown), Google receives information such as the device's IP address and the approximate location worked out from it, device type and operating system, device identifiers other than the advertising ID, the app, whether a video was watched or tapped, and diagnostic data such as crash and performance information, together with the child-directed and age settings above (not the age itself). It is sent encrypted. Google uses it to deliver and measure the ad, limit how often ads repeat, and prevent fraud, not to build a profile of your child. See Google's privacy policy and how Google uses information from apps that use its services.
  • On iPhone and iPad: provided by Kidoz. Kidoz is an ad network made only for children's apps. It shows contextual ads, chosen to match the game rather than your child, and is certified under PRIVO's COPPA Safe Harbor program. The game starts Kidoz only when a video is requested, never while videos are switched off, and never uses Apple's advertising identifier (IDFA) or shows Apple's tracking prompt.
  • What Kidoz receives. Each time a video is requested, Kidoz receives the technical information it needs to show and count the ad, such as a shortened (truncated) IP address, the device type and operating system, the app, and whether a video was watched or tapped. Kidoz says it does not collect personal identifiers, cookies or persistent IP addresses. See Kidoz's privacy policy.
  • Tapping an ad. If a player taps the ad itself during a video, it can open the app store or the advertiser's page, as with any mobile ad. On Android, players under 13 only get ads that Google has approved for children; on iPhone and iPad, every Kidoz ad is made for children.
  • Parents are in control. Switch reward videos off on the parents' page and the game stops requesting them. You can report any ad with "Report an ad".

If we add any other feature that sends data, we will update this policy first, tell you in the game before the change applies, and ask for verifiable parental consent wherever the law requires it.

Who we share data with

We hold no personal data, and we never sell data. The only outside services in the game are Google AdMob on Android and Kidoz on iPhone and iPad, which receive the information described under Reward videos directly from the device when a video is requested. We use them only for non-personalised ads (child-directed on Android for players under 13, contextual-only with Kidoz), and we choose outside services that protect data at least as well as this policy does. If we add another service, we will name it here first.

How long data is kept

Game progress, learning stats, the age answer and the video switch stay on the device until you tap Start the game over on the parents' page, or delete the app. Google's consent tool keeps an adult's ad privacy choice on the device until it is changed or the app is deleted. We keep nothing on our side. Google keeps the ad information it receives for the periods set out in its privacy policy.

Your choices and rights

  • See your child's data: open the parents' page in the game.
  • Delete it: tap Start the game over, or delete the app. Starting over resets the game's settings too, so check the video switch afterwards. See Delete data.
  • Turn videos off: switch reward videos off on the parents' page at any time, and the game stops requesting them.
  • Consent (adult players in the EU and UK only): where Google's consent rules apply to a player aged 18 or over, the game asks for a grown-up, shows the grown-up question, and then Google's consent message, before the first video. That adult can change the choice at any time with Ad privacy choices on the parents' page. Players under 18 never see a consent form; they only get limited, non-personalised ads, so there is nothing for a parent to agree to on a child's behalf.
  • Report an ad: tap Report an ad on the parents' page, or email orionlabstudios@gmail.com, if an ad seems wrong for children.
  • Data Google holds: we can't see or delete it, but you can use the choices in Google's privacy policy.
  • Questions or requests: email orionlabstudios@gmail.com. Parents and players in the EU, UK and Switzerland, California and other places with privacy laws have rights to access, correct and delete personal data and to complain to a data protection authority. Because we hold no personal data about you or your child, a request to us will usually confirm that we hold nothing.

Security

Data stays on the device, protected by the device's own security. Anyone who can use the device can open the game and the parents' page, so please keep your device's lock screen on.

Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we update the date at the top. For important changes, we tell you in the game before they apply.
